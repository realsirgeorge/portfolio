# Kenya's Cyber Crossroads: Securing the Silicon Savannah

Kenya's digital economy is one of Africa's most advanced — and one of its most targeted. In 2025, the Communications Authority detected **12.5 billion cyber threats**, a 247% jump from the previous year. That translates to roughly five cyberattacks per citizen every quarter. The Silicon Savannah has a serious security problem.

## What's Driving the Threat?

AI-powered scams are the fastest-growing vector. Fraudsters now clone the voices of bank staff and family members to steal money, while fake job scams on WhatsApp and TikTok exploit economic anxieties to harvest "registration fees." For businesses, social engineering — manipulating people rather than systems — remains the most common point of entry, with AI automating phishing at scale.

## Kenya's Response

The most significant structural move is the proposed **National Cybersecurity Agency (NCSA)**, backed by Cabinet and awaiting parliamentary approval. It would consolidate Kenya's fragmented cyber governance — currently spread across KE-CIRT/CC, NC4, the Data Protection Commissioner, and others — into a single, empowered authority.

On the international front, Kenya launched the **Kenya Cyber Resilience (KCR) Project** on January 26, 2026 — a €3 million EU-funded initiative running over 36 months. It targets three areas: legal and regulatory reform, operational incident response capacity, and nationwide public awareness campaigns focused on women, youth, and digital financial services users.

## Where Kenya Stands

The ITU ranks Kenya **21st globally and 3rd in Africa** for cybersecurity readiness — a strong position, but one with acknowledged gaps in enforcement and technical implementation. A critical skills shortage persists, with organisations paying 45% above regional averages for cybersecurity talent.

## The Road Ahead

Key milestones to watch: NCSA establishment, ratification of the Budapest Convention on Cybercrime, and formal Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) designations. Kenya has the frameworks. The test now is execution.

The Silicon Savannah has always leapfrogged. Cybersecurity may be its most consequential leap yet.
